## Slide 2: Introduction (Setting the Stage)
Headline: The Era of Generative AI & RAG
*   The Rise of LLMs: Tools like ChatGPT and Claude have revolutionized how we interact with information.
*   The Constraint: LLMs are trained on general knowledge, not *your* private data.
*   Enter RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation): The technique of connecting an LLM to your own documents (PDFs, Wikis, Databases) so it can answer specific questions.
*   The Crucial Step: Before an LLM can read your data, that data must be prepared. This is where Text Splitting comes in.

---

## Slide 3: The Problem (Why Chunking Matters)
Headline: LLMs Have a Limit
*   The Context Window: Models cannot read an infinite amount of text at once. They have a limit (e.g., 8k, 128k tokens).
*   The Garbage In, Garbage Out Rule: Feeding massive, unstructured documents leads to:
    *   Lost Information: The "Lost in the Middle" phenomenon.
    *   High Costs: Paying for unnecessary tokens.
    *   Hallucinations: The model making things up when overwhelmed.
*   The Solution: We need a way to break documents down intelligently.

---

## Slide 4: What is Text Splitting?
Headline: It's Not Just Cutting Strings
*   Definition: The process of dividing a large document into smaller, semantically meaningful units called "Chunks".
*   The Goal: To fit these chunks into an LLM's context window while preserving the meaning of the text.
*   The Challenge: Where do you cut?
    *   Cut in the middle of a sentence? -> Meaning lost.
    *   Cut between paragraphs? -> Better.
    *   Cut based on token count? -> Essential for API constraints.

---

## Slide 5: Key Concepts
Headline: The Anatomy of a Chunk
1.  Chunk Size:
    *   The maximum size of a single piece of text (e.g., 1000 characters).
    *   Trade-off: Too small = no context. Too big = hard to find specific answers.
2.  Chunk Overlap:
    *   The amount of text shared between the end of one chunk and the start of the next (e.g., 200 characters).
    *   Why? To ensuring that a sentence cut in half is fully preserved in at least one chunk.
3.  Separators:
    *   The logic used to split text (Newlines `\n\n`, Periods `.`, Spaces ` `, etc.).
